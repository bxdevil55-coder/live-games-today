Cricket Rules and Scoring: A Beginner's Guide

Cricket is a bat-and-ball sport played between two teams. In the common format, one team bats to score runs while the other team bowls and fields to restrict runs and dismiss batters.

1. What Is an Over?

An over normally consists of six legal deliveries bowled by one bowler. Certain illegal deliveries, such as wides and no-balls, do not count toward the six legal deliveries. The exact rules depend on the format and playing conditions.

2. How Are Runs Scored?

  • Running between wickets: Batters may score runs by completing runs between the wickets.
  • Four: Normally awarded when the ball reaches the boundary after touching the ground, subject to the Laws.
  • Six: Normally awarded when the batter hits the ball over the boundary without it first touching the ground.
  • Extras: Runs may be awarded for wides, no-balls, byes, and leg-byes, according to the rules.

3. What Is a Wicket?

In cricket, the word wicket can refer to the stumps and bails, the pitch in some contexts, or the dismissal of a batter. Scorecards commonly use wickets to show how many batters have been dismissed.

Common ways a batter may be dismissed include bowled, caught, leg before wicket (LBW), run out, and stumped. Each dismissal has specific conditions in the Laws of Cricket.

4. Understanding a Cricket Score

A score such as 150/4 means the team has scored 150 runs and lost four wickets. The overs figure shows how many overs have been completed and how many legal balls have been bowled in the current over.

For example, 19.3 overs means 19 completed overs plus three legal balls in the next over. It does not mean 19.3 decimal overs.

5. Main Cricket Formats

  • Test cricket: Usually scheduled over up to five days, with each team generally having two innings.
  • One Day International (ODI): Normally limited to 50 overs per team, subject to playing conditions.
  • Twenty20 (T20): Normally limited to 20 overs per team, subject to playing conditions.

6. What Is a Run Rate?

The run rate is the average runs scored per over. It is calculated by dividing the runs scored by the number of overs faced, using the actual number of legal balls when calculating partial overs.

For example, 60 runs from 10 completed overs gives a run rate of 6 runs per over.

7. How to Follow a Match

Check the score, wickets, overs, target, required run rate, and match status. In a chase, the target is the score the batting team needs to reach to win. Live score providers may occasionally update with a delay, so verify important results with the tournament's official source.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

What does all out mean?

It means the batting innings has ended because the team can no longer continue under the applicable rules, commonly after ten batters have been dismissed in a standard eleven-player side.

What does DLS mean?

The Duckworth–Lewis–Stern method is used in certain limited-overs matches affected by interruptions to calculate a revised target or result.
